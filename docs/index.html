Use cases

Postcards from where numbers come from

Numbers rarely start in your code. They arrive from shoppers, banks, scrapers and spreadsheets, written the way their sender writes them.

From São Paulo

Checkout and price fields

A shopper types 1.234,56 into your price field. normalizeInput keeps partial input valid while they type and hands you the number on blur.

From Berlin

CSV and bank exports

A bank export has 1.234,56 in one column and 1,5 in the next. createParser reads every row in one pass, and detect() takes files with no locale.

From Tokyo

Scraped and pasted prices

Prices arrive with symbols and codes on either side. parseCurrency returns the amount and the ISO code, so you know what you are adding up.

From London

Accounting reports

Negatives in parentheses, a trailing minus or the Unicode minus sign all read as negative, the way an accountant meant them.

From Mumbai

Money in integer cents

toMinorUnits shifts the decimal digits as text, so 0,29 is 29 cents and never 28.999999999999996. Lakh and crore grouping included.

From Cairo

Every script

Arabic-Indic, Persian, Devanagari, Bengali, Thai and full width digits read like Latin ones, with their own separators.

FAQ

Questions at the gate

Why not swap the comma for a dot?

Because the meaning of . and , depends on who wrote the number. Swapping fixes "1,5" and breaks "1.234,56" and "1,234". parse(text, locale) reads the separators of that locale from Intl.NumberFormat instead.

What does parseFloat do with 1.234,56?

It reads characters while they look like an English number and stops at the first one that does not, so parseFloat("1.234,56") is 1.234. A wrong number passes every isNaN check, which makes it worse than an error.

How do I get the currency of a price?

parseCurrency("R$ 1.234,56", "pt-BR") returns the amount 1234.56 and the code "BRL". Add toMinorUnits: true to get exact integer cents.

What if I do not know the locale?

Paste it in the box at the top of this page. For one value, detect(text) works out the decimal separator and says when it cannot be sure. For a column, parseColumn(values) lets the clear cells decide for the unclear ones.

Where does it run?

Node.js 18 and newer, Deno, Bun and modern browsers. It has zero dependencies, ships ESM, CommonJS and TypeScript types, and is about 7 kB gzipped.
